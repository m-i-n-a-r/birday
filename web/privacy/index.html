Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 24, 2026

Birday is an open source Android app to remember birthdays and other recurring events, built by an independent developer (m.i.n.a.r.). This page explains, in plain language, what data the app touches and what it does with it. The short version: Birday has no server backend, no account, and no analytics or advertising SDK, and it does not even hold the permission to access the Internet. Everything you save stays in the app's own private storage on your phone, and it leaves the device only when you export or share it yourself, or through Android's own backup, described below.

Network access

NetworkNone

Birday does not declare the INTERNET permission, so it has no way to send or receive anything over the network. The only network-related permission it lists is the ability to check whether a connection is available, added by Android's WorkManager library, which Birday uses to schedule background tasks; that permission cannot be used to transfer data. Links in the app, such as the Play Store page or the source code repository, open in your browser or in the store app, outside of Birday.

Contacts

Importing from your contactsOnly if you allow it

The contacts permission is requested only when you use a feature that needs it, and the app works without it. When granted, Birday reads the name parts of your contacts (prefix, first, middle and last name, suffix), the dates saved in them as birthdays, anniversaries or other events, together with their custom labels, and the contact photo, scaled down to a small thumbnail. Phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses and every other field are never read.

This happens when you import from contacts, when you type a name while adding an event (to suggest matching contacts), and when you open the details of an event (to find a contact with the same name and offer a button that opens it in your contacts app). Events and photos are saved in Birday only when you import them.

Automatic importOptional, off by default

If you turn on automatic import in the settings, Birday repeats the contacts import when you open the app, and a weekly background task does the same if the app has not been opened for a week. Turning the option off cancels the background task.

Calendar

Importing and exportingOnly when you ask

The calendar permissions are requested only when you import from or export to the calendar. Importing reads the events of the coming year from your phone's calendars (title, description, date and repetition rule), keeps only the ones that repeat every year, and shows them to you so you can pick which to add. Exporting creates a calendar named after the app on your device, as a local calendar that is not linked to any online account, and writes your events into it. A long press on the export option deletes that calendar.

Data that stays entirely on your device

These are kept locally to make the app work, and never leave your phone unless you move them yourself:

Your eventsLocal only

Names, dates, event types, notes, favorites and pictures are stored in a database in the app's private storage. Statistics and countdowns are computed on the device from those same entries.

Event picturesNo permission at all

To give an event a picture, you choose one through Android's system photo picker, which hands the app only the image you selected; no storage or media permission is requested. The image is cropped on the device, and the result is saved inside the event. The temporary copy made while cropping lives in the app's cache.

Notifications, alarms and widgetsLocal only

Reminders are scheduled on your device and posted by your phone, and they can include the names and the picture of the upcoming events (pictures can be hidden in the settings). The alarm and boot permissions only serve to refresh the widgets right after midnight and to restore that schedule after a restart. The widgets show your upcoming events on the home screen, so they are visible to anyone who can see your screen. Asking to ignore battery optimizations is optional and only makes reminders more reliable.

Your settingsLocal only

The theme, notification preferences and every other option are stored in the app's private storage. So are the launch counter and the date of first launch, used only to decide when to ask you to rate the app.

Quick appsNo permission at all

The quick apps panel shows shortcuts to a few messaging apps (WhatsApp, Telegram, Viber, Signal, Instagram, Messenger and Threema). To know which ones to show, Birday asks Android whether those specific apps are installed, and nothing else: it never sees the full list of apps on your phone. The dialer, messages and email shortcuts simply open those apps, and nothing is sent on your behalf.

Backups and exports

Files you exportOnly when you ask

You can export your events as a Birday backup, a JSON file or a CSV file. You choose where each file is saved through Android's system file picker, and after saving, the share sheet opens so you can send it wherever you want, if you want. The Birday backup is a copy of the whole database, pictures included, so keep these files somewhere you trust. Importing reads only the file you pick.

Automatic backupOptional, off by default

An experimental option saves a Birday backup automatically when the app is closed, in the location you picked for your last manual backup, or in the app's own folder on your device if you never picked one.

Android backupManaged by Android

Birday allows Android's built-in backup, so your events and settings can be included in your device backup and moved to a new phone during setup. Whether this happens depends on your phone's backup settings, not on Birday, and the backup is handled by Android and by the account you use for it, under that provider's policy. The developer has no access to it.

What Birday does not do

  • No account or sign-up, ever.
  • No advertising, no ad SDKs, no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics, telemetry, or crash-reporting SDK of any kind.
  • No Internet permission, so nothing can be sent to the developer or to anyone else.
  • No location access, and no access to your photos or files beyond what you hand it yourself.
  • No reading of phone numbers, email addresses or other contact details beyond names, dates and photos.
  • No selling or sharing of data with third parties, because none is collected.

Open source

Birday's source code is public and released under the GPL 3.0 license, so everything described on this page can be verified on GitHub.

Children's privacy

Birday is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone, of any age. The app has no account system and no network access, so there is no mechanism by which it could.

Data retention & deletion

Everything described above lives in your device's local storage. You can delete all your events from the experimental settings at any time, and uninstalling Birday (or clearing its app data from Android settings) permanently deletes all of it. Files you exported, the calendar created by the export and your device backups stay where they are, and can be removed from there. There is nothing on our end to delete, because nothing is stored on our end.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above will change accordingly. Meaningful changes will also be reflected in the app's release notes.
